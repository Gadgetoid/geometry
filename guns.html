GEOMETRY II GUNS

THE RIG

A hull held still with its guns live. Drag in the frame to put the target somewhere and it stays there. Each mount is drawn with the arc it covers, where it is pointed and where it is trying to point; a mount goes green the frame it is allowed to fire. Nothing else arrives, and nothing here can be lost.

EVERY MOUNT, THIS FRAME

BEFORE A SHOT GOES OUT

In order, for a gun that decides for itself. A shot that never appears failed one of these.

THE CONTROLLERS

A loadout entry names one. It is called once a frame for a ready weapon and decides whether to fire; the aimer beside it runs whether or not the gun is loaded, so a mount is already round when it comes ready.

WHO USES WHAT

ARCS, HULL BY HULL

Nose to the right. A wedge is what a mount can be brought to bear on, centred on arcFrom off the nose; a dashed ring traverses freely.

THE FIRING CYCLE

One line per weapon and the controller driving it, to a common scale. A banded segment is a range the gun rolls within each time.

WHAT THAT COMES TO

Per second, at the cycle above, with a burst counted at the rate it is rolled. Damage is one barrel on one target.

WHAT A HULL'S GUNS COST IT

Every gun firing its cycle at once, against the plant. Over 100% the cell is the limit and the guns fire slower than their reloads say.
